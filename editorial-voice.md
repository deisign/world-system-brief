# World System Brief — editorial-voice.md

> **Human editorial voice layer**
>
> Status: v0.1
>
> Product languages: **en / ua**
>
> This document does not define the analytical model.
>
> `brain.md` defines what WSB is.  
> `schema/` defines how WSB remembers.  
> `discourse.md` defines what WSB may claim and how analytical meaning survives language.  
> **`editorial-voice.md` defines how correct WSB prose remains recognizably human.**

---

# 1. Purpose

A structurally correct text can still be dead.

It can preserve every Δ, scope every claim, cite every source, avoid every forbidden cliché — and still sound like a machine that has learned the conventions of professional intelligence writing.

WSB does not accept that as finished prose.

The purpose of this layer is not to make WSB "literary." It is to prevent analytical discipline from producing synthetic, interchangeable language.

The target is:

~~~text
analytical precision
+ compression
+ human rhythm
+ an intelligent reader
+ controlled authorial presence
- generated smoothness
~~~

The publication should feel written by people who have a model of the world, not by a model that has a template for writing about the world.

---

# 2. Source and adaptation rule

This document is informed by a broader authorial voice corpus whose original register is cultural, conversational, ironic, associative, and image-rich.

WSB does **not** copy that voice wholesale.

## Transfer almost directly

- assume an intelligent reader;
- do not lecture;
- alternate sentence length;
- allow controlled roughness;
- let facts move the thought rather than decorate expertise;
- permit a visible editorial angle;
- distrust advertising, institutional and expert-performance language;
- use irony against empty seriousness;
- prefer a concrete object or mechanism to an abstract mood;
- let a paragraph turn rather than merely accumulate;
- allow a short sentence to land hard.

## Adapt in reduced form

- conversational turns;
- physical imagery;
- register shifts;
- dry humor;
- parenthetical asides;
- self-made labels for recurring phenomena;
- unexpected analogies;
- final sentences with residue.

## Do not transfer as requirements

- cultural genealogy for every object;
- obligatory irony;
- obligatory physical metaphor;
- obligatory final punchline;
- taste judgments as an editorial function;
- tenderness as a required register;
- deliberate profanity as a voice marker;
- treating every analytical object as a cultural artifact.

WSB may occasionally use these. It must never depend on them.

---

# 3. The reader is an equal

Assume the reader is intelligent but not necessarily pre-briefed.

Do not write:

> For those unfamiliar with the concept, it is important to understand that...

Prefer:

> In practice, this means...

Or simply explain the mechanism without announcing that an explanation is beginning.

Do not perform expertise at the reader. Do not congratulate the reader for understanding. Do not over-explain a point merely because another explanatory paragraph can be generated.

The desired relation is:

~~~text
not lecturer → student
not expert → layperson
not brand → audience

but

analyst ↔ intelligent reader
~~~

WSB can introduce difficult concepts without apologizing for their difficulty.

---

# 4. Human rhythm

Generated prose tends toward locally perfect, globally monotonous sentences.

WSB should have rhythm.

A useful pattern is:

~~~text
short frame
→ medium explanation
→ longer sentence carrying mechanism and qualification
→ short landing
~~~

This is not a template. Repetition of the pattern would itself become synthetic.

Sentence length should follow thought.

A short sentence may exist because the state is simple:

> The bottleneck moved.

A longer sentence may exist because the mechanism genuinely contains several linked conditions.

Do not split complex thought merely to simulate clarity. Do not lengthen simple thought to simulate sophistication.

---

# 5. Controlled roughness

Do not polish prose until every edge disappears.

Useful human roughness includes:

- an unexpected but exact verb;
- a compact aside;
- a sentence fragment when it carries information;
- an abrupt transition when the analytical turn is abrupt;
- a dry correction of the previous frame;
- a slightly asymmetrical paragraph;
- a phrase with memorable physical weight.

Roughness is not sloppiness.

It must never damage factual accuracy, scope, causality, uncertainty, semantic parity, or readability.

> **Remove friction that obscures the model. Keep friction that gives the thought shape.**

---

# 6. Facts must do work

A fact is not evidence of the writer's competence.

Every number, date, institution, capacity figure, rule or historical comparison should do at least one job:

1. establish previous or current state;
2. explain mechanism;
3. establish scale;
4. distinguish announcement from operation;
5. change confidence;
6. reveal dependency;
7. locate a constraint;
8. show adaptation;
9. identify rent movement;
10. establish that nothing material changed.

If a fact does none of these, ask why it is in the paragraph.

Do not build encyclopedic vestibules before reaching the point.

---

# 7. Authorial angle without opinion theatre

WSB is not neutral in the sense of having no editorial intelligence.

It chooses what counts as structural, which comparison matters, which dependency is hidden by the headline, which event is noise, and which missing fact prevents a stronger conclusion.

That analytical angle should be visible.

But WSB should not manufacture personality through arbitrary opinions.

Bad:

> This absurd policy is obviously doomed.

Good:

> The policy adds a new compliance layer but does not create alternative capacity.

Good:

> The announcement is large. The operational change is not — yet.

The voice comes from selection, precision, rhythm and judgment about evidence. Not from performative attitude.

---

# 8. Irony has a target

Irony is allowed.

Its preferred targets are empty seriousness, inflated institutional language, advertising claims, consultant abstractions, geopolitical theatre, ceremonial announcements mistaken for capacity, precision unsupported by evidence, and labels pretending to be mechanisms.

Irony should generally point upward at language, systems and claims — not downward at people caught inside them.

It must not turn Noise Check into snark.

A dry sentence is often enough:

> Three communiqués later, the pipeline capacity is the same.

The joke works because it is also the analytical result.

---

# 9. Physical language

Abstract systems become legible when some prose has physical structure.

Useful images often come from movement, pressure, blockage, distance, storage, queues, weight, pipes, gates, bridges, detours, layers, friction and time.

This is especially natural for WSB because the model already describes flows and constraints.

Good:

> The bottleneck moved downstream.

Good:

> The new route is a bypass, but a narrow one.

Bad:

> A tsunami of geopolitical uncertainty swept through the fragile architecture of global trade.

If the metaphor makes the system less measurable, delete it.

---

# 10. Register

WSB can move between technical, economic, regulatory, industrial, conversational and occasionally literary registers.

It should not sound trapped in any one of them.

A technical term can sit beside a plain sentence. A dense paragraph can end with four ordinary words. A diagram label can be austere while Chain of the Day carries more texture.

This is not stylistic inconsistency. Different parts of the interface perform different jobs.

The governing rule is precision, not uniformity.

---

# 11. Conversational markers

Conversational turns can make reasoning visible:

~~~text
in practice
more precisely
for now
the problem is
except that
not yet
the catch is
which matters because
at this point
so far
~~~

UA equivalents should be native rather than mechanically mirrored.

Use them when they expose a turn in thought. Do not sprinkle them to manufacture informality.

The human corpus is evidence about rhythm, not a phrase bank.

---

# 12. Parentheses and dashes

Dashes can carry correction, qualification, an analytical side-step, a compact contrast, or an abrupt state change.

Parentheses can carry secondary precision, a quiet correction, or a small aside.

Neither should become decoration.

If the aside contains the main analytical fact, it is not an aside.

If every paragraph contains a wink in parentheses, the wink becomes a tic.

---

# 13. Lists should have internal logic

A WSB list is not automatically clearer because it has bullets.

Use lists for genuine parallel structure. When prose is better, use prose.

Useful ordering may run general → specific, upstream → downstream, physical → regulatory, state → mechanism → consequence, or strong evidence → remaining uncertainty.

Do not use three bullets merely because three bullets look complete.

Do not force triads. Mechanical threes are a common generated-prose fingerprint.

---

# 14. Endings

Generated prose likes to summarize itself.

WSB should rarely end a section by repeating what the section just said.

Possible endings include a watch condition, unresolved constraint, short consequence, reversal, precise remaining uncertainty, or a line that changes the reader's frame.

Good:

> The plant exists. The substitute does not — yet.

Good:

> Next test: commercial throughput, not commissioning photos.

Good:

> The route changed. The dependence did not.

Do not force every ending to sparkle. Sometimes the best ending is simply the state.

---

# 15. Openings

Do not clear your throat.

Avoid:

> In today's rapidly changing global landscape...

> As geopolitical tensions continue to evolve...

> There are several important developments worth noting...

Start with the Δ, surprising non-Δ, mechanism, number that changes interpretation, or system condition.

Examples:

> Four tracked relations changed today. Nine loud events did not.

> Shipping cost moved before physical flow did.

> Europe added refining capacity. It did not yet add a substitute.

The first sentence should already belong to this issue.

---

# 16. Naming phenomena

A publication develops intelligence partly by naming recurring structures.

WSB may create compact labels for phenomena not well served by standard terminology.

Provisional examples of the kind of naming allowed:

~~~text
announcement capacity
paper bypass
regulatory bottleneck migration
dependency laundering
headline Δ
~~~

A coined label must describe a repeatable mechanism, survive outside one joke, be understandable from context, not replace a more precise established term, and earn repeated use.

Naming is analytical compression, not branding theatre.

---

# 17. Humor

Humor is a mode of thought, not a quota.

The strongest WSB humor will often come from collision between grand claim and unchanged physical fact, or ceremonial language and actual system state.

Example:

> The strategy has entered its third name. Capacity remains at zero.

No joke needs to be added.

Do not write punchlines around suffering, war casualties, disasters, or vulnerable people.

---

# 18. Elegance is allowed

Analytical prose does not need to sound like appliance documentation.

Chain of the Day may be beautifully written. A structural explanation may have cadence. A metaphor may be memorable. A sentence may be worth quoting.

The restriction is not against style.

It is against style that substitutes for mechanism, evidence, scope, causality or uncertainty.

> **Style may carry the model. It may not impersonate the model.**

---

# 19. Anti-smoothness

A dangerous WSB draft is one that is correct, fluent, balanced, professional — and completely interchangeable with a hundred AI-generated research briefs.

Warning signs:

- every paragraph is the same length;
- every paragraph begins with a topic sentence;
- every paragraph ends with a mini-conclusion;
- transitions announce rather than perform thought;
- every contrast is symmetrical;
- every list has three items;
- every section politely explains its importance;
- no sentence could have been written only for this particular fact pattern;
- deleting 20% changes nothing.

The cure is not artificial eccentricity.

The cure is stronger editing.

---

# 20. Compression test

After the analytical draft is correct, ask:

> What can disappear without losing the model?

Delete it.

Then ask:

> What became too compressed to understand?

Restore only that.

WSB should be dense because the world is dense, not because the prose is crowded.

---

# 21. Replace generated rhetoric with observation

Generated:

> This underscores the growing importance of supply-chain resilience.

WSB:

> The alternative route now carries part of the tracked flow; terminal capacity has become the binding constraint.

Generated:

> Taken together, these developments point to a broader shift.

WSB:

> Two constraints moved downstream; the underlying import dependence did not.

Generated:

> The key takeaway is that the situation remains complex.

WSB:

> Refining eased. Licensing tightened.

The replacement is usually shorter because the model contains more information than the rhetoric.

---

# 22. The McKinsey / Reuters / LLM test

A useful diagnostic:

> **If a WSB paragraph can be pasted without loss into a generic consultancy report, a routine wire explainer, or a standard LLM research brief, it is not finished.**

This does not mean WSB must be eccentric.

It means the paragraph should contain enough specific analytical structure that removing it from WSB removes something.

Ask:

- Is there a real Δ?
- Is the relation specific?
- Is the mechanism visible?
- Is the sentence rhythm serving this exact thought?
- Is there an observation that belongs to this fact pattern?
- Is there unnecessary professional filler?

If the prose survives everywhere, it probably belongs nowhere.

---

# 23. Sterility test

Another diagnostic:

> **Do not make the text smooth, enthusiastic, sterile and safe.**

"Safe" here does not mean reckless claims.

It means prose edited until no human choice remains visible.

WSB should remain rigorous and cautious where evidence requires caution. It should not become timid in syntax, observation or form.

# 24. Relationship to discourse.md

When this document conflicts with `discourse.md`, **`discourse.md` wins on analytical meaning**.

Hierarchy:

~~~text
EVIDENCE
   ↓
LEDGER STATE
   ↓
DISCOURSE CONTRACT
   ↓
EDITORIAL VOICE
   ↓
FINAL PROSE
~~~

Voice may change how a claim is expressed.

Voice may never change Δ direction, confidence, actor, relation, time horizon, causal strength, scope, quantitative values, or status.

---

# 25. Relationship to EN / UA

EN and UA do not need identical voice mechanics.

The same human quality may require different syntax.

Do not reproduce dash frequency, parenthesis frequency, sentence length, discourse markers or humor mechanically across languages.

Preserve analytical identity. Then edit each language until it sounds written in that language.

> **Translate the model. Re-author the sentence.**

---

# 26. LLM workflow

LLMs may help produce WSB prose.

They are drafting machinery, not the publication's authorial identity.

Preferred pipeline:

~~~text
structured state
   ↓
draft
   ↓
fact / Δ / scope / confidence QA
   ↓
discourse.md lint
   ↓
LLM_FINGERPRINT lint
   ↓
editorial-voice pass
   ↓
EN / UA native-language pass
   ↓
publication
~~~

The editorial-voice pass asks a different question from factual QA:

> Does this sound like someone actually thought this sentence?

---

# 27. Human-corpus rule

A curated human corpus may be used to study WSB voice.

Use it to extract rhythm, compression, syntactic variation, transitions, treatment of the reader, degree of explicitness, use of irony, physicality of language, endings, and avoidance patterns.

Do **not** use it as a phrase bank, vocabulary sampler, prompt for superficial imitation, license to copy idiosyncratic tics, or reason to inject slang where it does not belong.

The corpus should teach the system how human editorial choices differ from generated defaults.

It should not turn WSB into an impersonation.

---

# 28. Editorial voice QA

Before publication, ask:

## Humanity

- Does the text have a thought, or merely a structure?
- Is any sentence present only because professional prose expects it?
- Is the rhythm monotonous?
- Are there mechanical triads?
- Are contrasts formulaic?
- Are transitions doing work?
- Did the draft explain something twice?
- Does the ending add anything?

## Specificity

- Could this paragraph belong to another story with nouns swapped?
- Does the prose expose the mechanism?
- Is there a sentence unique to this fact pattern?
- Are abstractions standing where an object, institution, flow or constraint could stand?

## Restraint

- Is irony helping analysis?
- Is an image clarifying rather than decorating?
- Is conversational language natural?
- Is the authorial presence stronger than the evidence?
- Is the prose trying to be quotable?

## Anti-generation

- Does this sound recognizably LLM-generated?
- Is it suspiciously smooth?
- Is it suspiciously balanced?
- Is there generic meta-language?
- Is there a canned takeaway?
- Is there a fake profound contrast?
- Could 20% disappear?

If yes, edit again.

---

# 29. Canonical voice tests

## Test A — generic professionalism

Fail:

> Taken together, these developments underscore the growing importance of supply-chain diversification in an increasingly complex geopolitical environment.

Pass:

> Two suppliers were added. Refining remains concentrated in the same node.

## Test B — fake drama

Fail:

> A seismic shift is reshaping the global battery landscape.

Pass:

> Cell assembly moved. Cathode dependence did not.

## Test C — over-explanation

Fail:

> It is important to understand that commissioning a facility does not necessarily mean that it is operating at full commercial scale.

Pass:

> Commissioned is not the same as producing at scale.

## Test D — sterile caution

Fail:

> While the development may be significant, further data will be required before definitive conclusions can be drawn.

Pass:

> Direction clear. Scale unverified.

## Test E — forced personality

Fail:

> Well, folks, the great supply-chain circus rolls on.

Pass:

> The policy changed. The cargo did not.

---

# 30. Final rule

The WSB voice should make analytical intelligence **felt without performing intelligence**.

It should be possible to be:

~~~text
precise without sounding bureaucratic
complex without sounding academic
confident without pretending certainty
skeptical without becoming cynical
stylish without decorating the evidence
human without performing humanity
~~~

The publication is finished when the prose no longer advertises either the institution or the machinery that produced it.

> **The model should be visible. The generator should not.**
