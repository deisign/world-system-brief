# The bypass has a bottleneck now

The useful number this morning is not the price of Brent. It is the amount of oil finding its way back through the Gulf.

Preliminary September data show a substantial recovery in Middle Eastern crude exports. That is not the same thing as a return to the old system. Saudi Arabia's East–West route toward Yanbu was damaged, more barrels shifted back toward Gulf terminals and Hormuz, and ship-to-ship transfer outside the strait became a larger part of the workaround.

Then the workaround began to fill up.

Gulf of Oman STS operations require ships, time, support services and usable water. As the operation scales, those inputs become constraints. Kpler's analysis points toward West Coast India and Malaysia as possible additional release valves, but both imply longer cycles and greater tanker demand.

**Saudi production → East–West × → Gulf terminals → Hormuz × → Oman STS → Asia ↪**

One bottleneck was bypassed. The bypass was damaged. Traffic shifted back through the original bottleneck. Another workaround expanded beyond it. Now the constraint is beginning to migrate there.

No mastermind is required. Pipes, ports, tankers and arithmetic are enough.
