# AI power hits the turbine queue

The scarcity behind the AI buildout is no longer only chips, GPUs or funding. A Reuters investigation published on **9 November** describes a global rush for **gas turbines and other dispatchable power equipment**. Developers seek refurbished machines and retired aircraft engines because new industrial turbine deliveries can stretch toward **2030–31**. That is a market report about an existing bottleneck, **not** a verified new physical change on 9 November.

## 1. A hard constraint between capital and computation

Reuters reports Wood Mackenzie estimates of more than **100 gigawatts of gas-turbine orders** against about **60–70 GW of estimated annual manufacturing capacity**. These quantities must not be subtracted mechanically: the order book is a *stock of demand*, while annual manufacturing is a *flow of capacity*. Nor does the comparison itself measure what percentage of orders are due for AI datacentres.

US delivery conversations extend to 2030–31, according to a quoted analyst. Used industrial turbines, reconditioned machines and jet-engine-derived generators can partially bypass original-equipment queues, but supply on that secondary market is itself finite. Connection permissions, grid upgrades, air permits and fuel remain separate obstacles even after equipment is procured.

**System reading:** The bottleneck sits outside the data-centre building, in a specialised manufacturing chain. Capital spending does not instantly convert into firm electrical supply. A project may substitute equipment type but cannot infer a universal substitution horizon from anecdotal lead times.

Source — Reuters, 9 November: https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/ai-power-rush-sends-data-centres-hunting-used-turbines-jet-engines-2026-11-09/

## 2. NextDC: a corporate pause reveals a second gate

On **9 November**, Australia's NextDC said it would stop work on projects that lack customer contracts after the delayed M3 Melbourne expansion faced power and community obstacles. This is a **reported company decision**, not an independently quantified reduction in operating national generation or data-centre computing capacity. The report did not establish the number of projects suspended or how many commissioned megawatts were taken offline.

**System reading:** Power connection, social licence and signed demand commitments interact with hardware supply. A turbine does not guarantee a site permit; a potential customer does not guarantee a live power connection. These are distinct gates, not a single scarcity percentage.

Source — Reuters, 9 November: https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/nextdc-pauses-uncontracted-projects-after-australian-data-centre-woes-2026-11-09/

## 3. Hormuz: disputed damage and a price signal are not reopening data

Reuters reported that on **9 November** an unnamed Iranian Revolutionary Guards Navy official said navigational aids had been destroyed in the southern Strait of Hormuz and no unauthorised vessels transited overnight. The source did **not** establish when or by whom navigation equipment was damaged, or independently verify traffic effects.

On the same morning, oil weakened as US remarks suggested less likelihood of immediate renewed strikes. A price change can register market expectations without confirming actual tonnage through a maritime chokepoint. Do not convert attributed damage claims or market hopes into a newly verified corridor closure or reopening.

Sources — Reuters, 9 November: https://www.reuters.com/world/iran-says-no-unauthorised-vessels-transited-strait-hormuz-overnight-after-2026-11-09/ ; Reuters (republished by Euronext), 9 November: https://live.euronext.com/en/financial-news/oil-falls-trump-signals-restraint-iran-amid-fresh-hormuz-shipping-attacks

## SYSTEM LEDGER · Seven dimensions

**Flow:** Electricity and gas-turbine manufacturing inputs for AI computation; datacentre project development; maritime energy deliveries.

**Dependency:** Data-centre construction depends on electrical connection and reliable generation, specialised turbine manufacturing, fuel access, permits and viable customer contracts.

**Bottleneck:** High turbine order backlogs, long original-equipment lead times, constrained refurbished equipment and separately constrained grid/permit access.

**Substitutability:** Used industrial turbines, aeroderivative engines and gas-engine generator sets are reported alternatives. The sources do not establish a verified common replacement schedule for all installations; retain **unknown**.

**Leverage:** Scarce turbine suppliers and grid/permit gatekeepers may command negotiating leverage. The reports do not quantify firm-specific causal market power.

**Rent:** Reports of second-hand equipment price increases are not a complete measure of scarcity rent or allocation of earnings. Keep the distribution **unknown**.

**Adaptation:** Refurbishment and engine conversion are observed procurement strategies, while NextDC's development pause is an announced company response. No new operating capacity is assumed.
