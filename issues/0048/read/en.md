# THE WAIVER IS LIVE. HORMUZ IS NOT SAFE.

By **16 November** a US extension of Jones Act exceptions for eligible fuel shipping had entered into effect, while traders discussed a global oil surplus and Iran announced new tanker and mine incidents around Hormuz. These are **three different evidence states**: enforceable transport rules, market forecasts, and disputed battlefield reports. Nothing here proves that one additional approved US voyage occurred today or that tanker passage through Hormuz is safe. [WSB-0048-SRC-01; WSB-0048-SRC-02]

## 1. A legal window actually takes effect

PortNews cites a US Customs and Border Protection notice confirming the **90-day extension effective 16 November** for specified energy and other cargoes under the Jones Act exception. Foreign-flagged vessels still require **individual voyage decisions** and checks for qualified US vessels. The loading deadline in the published account is **14 February 2027**. This is a verified **change in policy status** from announcement on 13 November to validity on the 16th, not certification of any new ship movement. [WSB-0048-SRC-02]

## 2. Oil can trade cheaper while maritime danger persists

Reuters reported at **11:14 GMT** Brent at **$95.32** and WTI at **$88.29**. It cited the IEA expectation for **105 million barrels/day** of fourth-quarter supply, around **1.7 million barrels/day more than projected demand**. These are spot prices and a quarterly forecast, respectively. Iran's Revolutionary Guards simultaneously alleged mine strikes and tanker fires on Sunday and Monday; **Reuters could not independently verify** the account. Neither allegation nor a lower risk premium is a measured safe-transit capacity. [WSB-0048-SRC-01]

## 3. Refinery statistics and reserve releases have separate clocks

Chinese official figures published Monday showed **October refinery throughput down 2% from September and 8.1% from a year earlier**, Reuters reported. This is past physical activity, not a new refinery stoppage on 16 November. The IEA also described future releases of emergency oil products; Reuters said **71 million barrels** would be available by late February, including **15 million barrels of diesel**. A **scheduled release** must not be treated as fuel already handed to a retailer. [WSB-0048-SRC-01; WSB-0048-SRC-03]

## SYSTEM LEDGER · Seven dimensions

**Flows:** US domestic legal coastal shipping options, world crude exports and prices, October China refinery runs, planned emergency diesel reserves.

**Dependencies:** Permission depends on voyage clearance and suitable carriers; safe Gulf shipment requires navigation, insurance and risk acceptance.

**Bottlenecks:** Individual vessel approval; Hormuz incidents and uncertainty; insufficient refined diesel despite crude balance improvement.

**Substitutability:** Foreign-flag vessels may expand US carriage where approved; future emergency stocks do not directly substitute completed deliveries.

**Leverage:** Regulators control vessel approvals; risk owners and insurers affect shipping, but magnitude of new leverage unmeasured.

**Rent:** Shipping and energy price spreads may redistribute margins without established same-day rent capture.

**Adaptation:** Jones Act extension becomes effective; planned reserve releases remain future and unverified mine reports remain attributed.
