# Two Gulfs, one oil market

Oil supply is recovering in one Gulf and contracting in another.

In the Persian Gulf, producers continue to move crude through alternative routes while the Strait of Hormuz remains hazardous. The recovery is real, but so is its dependence on bypass capacity. More barrels moving through the system does not mean the original constraint has disappeared.

Across the Atlantic, the Gulf of Mexico has moved from forecast risk to physical interruption. Storm-related shutdowns affected 25.08% of offshore oil production and 16.37% of offshore gas production, according to figures reported for 7 October. Shell and Chevron confirmed production curtailments.

The distinction in dates matters. The shutdowns were already under way on 7 October. The evidence available for this edition establishes the transition that yesterday's brief still classified as developing. This is a confirmed change in the system, recorded with late evidence rather than assigned an invented start date.

The two developments act on the same global market but through different mechanisms. Middle Eastern producers are recovering export capacity by changing routes. US Gulf operators are temporarily withdrawing production because of weather. The first is adaptation to a persistent chokepoint. The second is an actual interruption of physical supply.

Emergency inventories provide another layer. The IEA is advancing implementation of the G7 stock-release decision, with diesel prioritised. That strengthens the existing buffer; it is not a second policy delta.

Competition for Asian buyers is also intensifying as Middle Eastern exports recover. This may eventually redistribute market share and pricing power. The available evidence supports a developing commercial trajectory, not a separately confirmed transition today.

Reports of US–Russia discussions about Nord Stream investment deserve attention for a different reason: they concern potential future control of infrastructure and rents. The pipelines remain inactive, sanctions remain relevant, and there is no confirmed restart. Talks are not gas flows.
