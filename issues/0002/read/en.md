# The spare route becomes an asset

Nothing new is flowing through LNG Canada today because of today's decision.

That is precisely why the decision matters.

The partners behind LNG Canada have taken final investment decision on Phase 2. The expansion adds another 14 million tonnes a year of liquefaction capacity at Kitimat, taking the project's total capacity from 14 to 28 mtpa when the new trains are built.

Yesterday, Phase 2 was an option. Today, it is committed infrastructure.

**Western Canadian gas → Kitimat LNG ┄┄↗┄▷ Pacific route ┄┄▷ Asian LNG market**

The dashed line matters. Phase 2 is not operating capacity, and WSB will not draw it as one. No present LNG dependency fell today. No cargo acquired a shorter route because a board approved capital expenditure.

What changed is the set of future choices.

Kitimat sits on Canada's Pacific coast. Its geography gives Canadian gas a direct route into Asian LNG markets without sending the cargo through the Gulf of Mexico, the Panama Canal or the Atlantic. A second 14 mtpa block therefore buys something more specific than “more LNG”: future export substitutability on a different piece of the map.

That distinction has acquired weight. The current Gulf disruption has made the cost of concentrated routes unusually visible. Yesterday's brief followed crude as one bypass filled and another constraint formed around it. Today's confirmed change is slower. It is capital being committed to another route before the molecules need it.

## Europe is looking at its own gate

The EU's methane rules are supposed to add reporting requirements for foreign oil and gas suppliers from January 2027. The European Commission is now considering moving that requirement to January 2028.

The pressure is straightforward. Europe wants lower methane emissions from imported energy. It also wants enough suppliers willing and able to sell into Europe while energy supply remains tight. Compliance can become a market-access constraint before it becomes an emissions result.

So the relation under watch is not “Europe versus methane.” It is narrower:

**tight supply → fewer comfortable substitutes → methane compliance § → possible delay ↘**

For now, the law has not moved. A proposal to loosen a gate is not an open gate.

**WESTERN CANADIAN GAS → LIQUEFACTION → PACIFIC SHIPPING → ASIAN LNG MARKET**

There are two clocks in this chain.

One measures cargoes. On that clock, today's FID reads zero.
