# The spare route becomes an asset

Today's decision added no gas to LNG Canada.

But Phase 2 is now going to be built. The LNG Canada partners have taken final investment decision on the expansion. Once the new trains are operating, Kitimat's capacity is expected to rise from 14 to 28 million tonnes of LNG a year.

Yesterday, Phase 2 was a project the partners could still walk away from. Today, money is committed to it.

**Western Canadian gas → LNG Canada ┄┄↗┄▷ Pacific route ┄┄▷ Asian LNG market**

Hence the dashed line. The extra 14 mtpa does not exist as physical flow yet. No current dependency weakened today and no new cargo started moving. What appeared is a future route that now has capital behind it.

Kitimat is on Canada's Pacific coast. Canadian gas can leave from there for Asia without crossing the Gulf of Mexico, the Panama Canal or the Atlantic. Phase 2 therefore adds more than volume. It adds another way to reach the same large market.

The current trouble in the Gulf makes the value of that geography easier to see. WSB-0001 followed oil around one constraint and into another. This change works on a slower clock: the route is being prepared years before it may be needed.

## The EU may postpone its own rule

From January 2027, foreign oil and gas suppliers are due to face new EU methane-reporting requirements. The European Commission is considering pushing that date back to January 2028.

Two practical aims are colliding. The EU wants to squeeze methane emissions out of imported-fuel supply chains. It also has less room to discard suppliers while energy supply is tight. An extra condition for market access becomes awkward when alternatives are scarce.

**tight supply → fewer substitutes → EU requirements § → possible delay ↘**

Nothing has been postponed yet. Discussing the date is not the same as changing the rule.
