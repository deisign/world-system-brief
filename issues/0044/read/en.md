# ACCESS ON PAPER IS NOT DELIVERED SUPPLY

The day's common mechanism is a gap between **a legal right to obtain a resource** and **the ability to move or use it**. ASEAN is considering pooled emergency fuel purchase rights; a Polish company has exited one Qatari LNG contract after non-delivery; and a tanker carrying Russian diesel lacks an assured US buyer despite sanctions relief. Separate evidence from Irish alumina exports and Chinese exploration shows why reporting a supply chain, a disputed end use or a test-well rate does not establish a new operational flow. These cases are not one causal chain. [WSB-0044-SRC-01; WSB-0044-SRC-02; WSB-0044-SRC-03; WSB-0044-SRC-04; WSB-0044-SRC-05]

## 1. ASEAN: a right to buy fuel is not an emergency stock release

On **12 November**, Reuters, citing Japan's Nikkei, reported that ASEAN members are preparing a joint diesel and gasoline stockpiling mechanism that could begin **as early as 2027**. The first proposed arrangement would allow the **Philippines** to buy products held in **Indonesia and Thailand** during emergencies. Rather than moving a nationally owned reserve to every country, a participant would pay upfront for a ticket granting a right to buy specified stock when needed. Letters of intent between Philippine PNOC affiliates and tank operators were **expected on Saturday, 14 November**, not already signed at the Thursday editorial cutoff. [WSB-0044-SRC-01]

**System reading:** an option on stored products can reduce procurement uncertainty without relocating the inventory. But its value depends on verified volumes, tank availability, ownership and legal release rules, refined-fuel quality and transport during a crisis. Neither actual reserve volumes nor commissioned cross-border release capacity are established by today's report. The target date is not a substitute for an implementation record.

[Reuters / Yuka Obayashi, 12 Nov — ASEAN ticket proposal](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/asean-launch-joint-oil-product-stockpiling-scheme-early-2027-nikkei-reports-2026-11-12/).

## 2. LNG contracts and diesel waivers: permission does not deliver cargo

**Orlen** announced on **10 November**, not today, that it had issued notice to end **one** long-term LNG contract with **QatarEnergy**, covering about **1 million tonnes per year** and otherwise running until 2034. The stated reason was ongoing force majeure and unfulfilled deliveries. The agreement remains in force for **60 days after notice**; another Qatari contract remains. This is an observable **contractual change dated 10 November**, not evidence of a new physical change on 12 November or a total collapse of Orlen's LNG supply. [WSB-0044-SRC-02]

A different example appears in Reuters reporting dated **11 November**: a tanker carrying Russian diesel was off the US East Coast, but American buyers and their banks remained reluctant to accept it despite a temporary US sanctions waiver. Legal scope, possible import restrictions, financing exposure and fuel specifications are separate gates. The report does not establish a buyer, completed US import, discharge or a new global route for that cargo. [WSB-0044-SRC-03]

**System reading:** contract enforceability, sanctions permission and vessel location are upstream conditions, not proof that the end-user can obtain usable molecules. The physical, commercial and legal chains must be measured separately.

[Reuters, 10 Nov — Orlen and QatarEnergy](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/polands-orlen-ends-long-term-qatarenergy-lng-contract-over-delivery-failure-2026-11-10/) · [Reuters, 11 Nov — Russian diesel tanker](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/us-companies-shunning-russian-diesel-tanker-carrying-that-fuel-idles-off-east-2026-11-11/).

## 3. Industrial materials: a verified input does not settle its final use

An Associated Press investigation published **12 November** describes continued exports of **alumina** from Russian-owned **Aughinish** in Ireland to Russian ports and smelters. Activists estimate **491,307 tonnes** shipped from March through October; that estimate is attributed, not an independently audited WSB total. The industrial connection to Russian aluminium processing is more supportable than claims about the exact military end use of each shipment. Irish officials and the refinery dispute or say they cannot establish those downstream claims, while investigators and the EU sanctions envoy express contrary concern. **No new EU export ban is verified on 12 November.** [WSB-0044-SRC-04]

**System reading:** there are two separable questions: whether the feedstock moves to an industrial processor and which customers ultimately receive the aluminium. An input flow cannot be equated with demonstrated use in a named weapon system without traceable downstream records.

[Sam McNeil and Jill Lawless, Associated Press, 12 Nov — alumina flows](https://apnews.com/article/ireland-russia-ukraine-war-alumina-sanctions-75d388cc6e92cc97341c13eb8b0e7ce6).

## 4. Bohai: the well-test rate is not a delivery contract

**CNOOC** announced on **12 November** that a Bohai Sea exploratory well drilled to about **5,770 metres** had encountered over **120 metres** of gas-bearing zones; it reported a test yield of roughly **800,000 cubic metres per day**. This is a company-reported **single-well testing rate**. It is not verified continuous field production, a certified recoverable resource volume, completed pipeline infrastructure or booked commercial gas delivery. [WSB-0044-SRC-05]

**System reading:** a geological option may be materially useful, but the transition from discovery to commercial capacity still requires development decisions, facilities, drilling, processing, connections and time. None of those operational milestones is proven by the well test.

[Reuters / Sam Li and Aizhu Chen, 12 Nov — CNOOC Bohai](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/chinas-cnooc-reports-major-ultra-deep-gas-discovery-bohai-sea-2026-11-12/).

## SYSTEM LEDGER · Seven dimensions

**Flows:** Emergency refined-product stock options in ASEAN; contracted and disrupted LNG supply; potentially marketable Russian diesel; existing Irish alumina shipments; a Chinese test-well gas flow that is not commercial supply.

**Dependencies:** The usable flow depends on stored inventory, emergency release permissions, shipping, surviving contractual obligations, sanctions and banking compliance, buyer acceptance and commissioned processing infrastructure.

**Bottlenecks:** An unimplemented ASEAN ticket system; undelivered Qatari LNG despite long-term contracting; US buyer and bank reluctance to handle Russian diesel; unresolved final-use traceability; the gap between CNOOC testing and field development.

**Substitutability:** Other suppliers and routes may exist, but the cited reports do not measure complete equivalent inventory, reliable replacement capacity, cost or a universal substitution horizon. Orlen reports other sources; that is not a quantified one-for-one replacement for the cancelled contract.

**Leverage:** Holders of reserve-release rights, suppliers able to fulfil LNG obligations, banks and customs authorities can affect commercial access. No evidence supports a new numeric leverage ranking today.

**Rent:** Ticket fees, LNG repricing, freight, sanctions compliance and alternative aluminium sourcing may redistribute costs, but no verified 12 November net-rent calculation is available.

**Adaptation:** ASEAN discusses cross-border stock options; Orlen exercised an existing legal exit on 10 November; market participants may avoid an import despite a waiver; alumina users face calls for sanctions; CNOOC explores a new resource. None of these is a newly verified system-wide physical delivery increase on 12 November.

## WHAT CHANGED — AND WHAT DID NOT

Five **new source-anchored descriptions of relationships** enter WSB's knowledge graph, but they are neither five new physical transitions nor proof of causal transmission between otherwise independent cases. The ASEAN scheme is prospective; Orlen's notice is dated **10 November**; the diesel tanker account is **11 November**; the AP supply-chain evidence and CNOOC report were published **12 November**.
