# A NEW GAS FIELD, AN OLD NETWORK

Shell said on **15 November** that Egypt's Mina West field has begun gas production. It feeds into an existing pipeline and processing system rather than waiting for an entirely new export network. The operator estimates possible capacity of **160 million cubic feet a day**, but did not disclose metered first-day output or the exact startup timestamp. A separate Reuters report on Yemen documents military repositioning near Bab el-Mandeb, which must not be converted into a measured fall in merchant traffic. [WSB-0047-SRC-01; WSB-0047-SRC-02]

## 1. Mina West: the difference between connected and merely discovered

Shell's announcement marks a real change from development to production: **a new Egyptian gas field is now producing and connected to the West Delta Deep Marine system**. Mina West was discovered in 2023. Shell operates the field with a **60% interest**; KUFPEC's subsidiary owns **40%**. The connection to pre-existing infrastructure shortens the development path but does not mean all capacity has already been reached. Reported potential: **160 million cubic feet per day**; confirmed actual daily output: **not specified**. [WSB-0047-SRC-01]

## 2. Why the connection matters more than the headline rate

Egypt's gas output had fallen substantially: Reuters cites Joint Organisations Data Initiative figures of **112.6 billion cubic feet in August 2026**, around **48% below August 2021**. A field flowing into existing gathering and processing networks offers domestic supply without proof of an entirely new trunk pipeline. The bottlenecks are field deliverability, processing and sustained capacity; no verified percentage of Egypt's shortfall is covered by Mina West on 15 November. [WSB-0047-SRC-01]

## 3. Bab el-Mandeb: military control and merchant throughput are different

Reuters also reported that Saudi-backed Yemeni government troops retreated from the Bab el-Mandeb area to reinforce Aden. This raises **maritime security exposure**, but the report does not count ships diverted that day or document a quantified reduction in trade throughput. Houthi claims about strikes on Saudi energy installations must remain attributed rather than turned into a confirmed new East-West Pipeline outage. [WSB-0047-SRC-02]

## SYSTEM LEDGER · Seven dimensions

**Flows:** New domestic Egyptian gas production connected to existing infrastructure; separately, threatened Red Sea maritime trade routes.

**Dependencies:** Production requires the Mina West reservoir, completed tie-in, existing West Delta Deep Marine processing, and domestic consumption links.

**Bottlenecks:** Realised well output and system deliverability; Bab el-Mandeb security cannot be inferred from transit volumes.

**Substitutability:** An existing trunk network accelerates tie-in. The field is not a complete measured substitute for Egypt's historic production losses.

**Leverage:** The operator controls production ramp-up; regional military actors can threaten routes, but a numeric new leverage shift is not evidenced.

**Rent:** Field and network sharing may change production and import costs; Shell/KUFPEC rent distribution is not quantified.

**Adaptation:** Shell reports commissioning by 15 November. Security movements in Yemen are observed separately from shipping impacts.
