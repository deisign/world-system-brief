# SHIPPING LAW IS NOT SHIPPING CAPACITY

The United States' additional 90-day Jones Act waiver was announced on **13 November** and is scheduled to take effect **16 November**. The Saturday edition closes the interval between announcement and actual validity. The measure widens the *legal possibility* of moving fuels between US ports on foreign-flag vessels, but it does not certify a particular tanker, a shipment or lower pump prices. [WSB-0046-SRC-01; WSB-0046-SRC-02]

## 1. The waiver is extended; voyage permission remains individual

Reuters reported on Friday that the administration continued the waiver for another 90 days. Unlike the earlier blanket arrangement, approval remains **case by case**: vessel, route, cargo and lack of suitable domestic carriage matter. The extension was publicly decided on **13 November**, not as an event newly enacted on the 14th. Its effective period starts on the 16th. A planned administrative window is not automatically utilised capacity. [WSB-0046-SRC-01; WSB-0046-SRC-02]

## 2. Historic volumes are not today's extra barrels

The White House said the waivers since March had facilitated roughly **90 million barrels** of additional US coastwise petroleum movement, including gasoline and crude. This is an **attributed, cumulative historical figure**, not a count of voyages or gallons shipped on Saturday and not an independent WSB audit. Extra ship eligibility could reduce a domestic distribution bottleneck without increasing refining or guaranteeing diesel availability. [WSB-0046-SRC-01]

## 3. The constraint moves from law to implementation

Without the waiver, the Jones Act normally reserves US domestic maritime cargo movement for compliant US vessels. Under the exception, eligibility remains conditional on review and actual operators' willingness. The constraint therefore changes form: a legal barrier can weaken while freight, terminal availability and product shortage remain. Whether consumers benefit requires measured authorised sailings, delivered product and prices, none established for **14 November**. [WSB-0046-SRC-02]

## SYSTEM LEDGER · Seven dimensions

**Flows:** Potential US coastal movements of petroleum and other eligible covered products; no new Saturday cargo count.

**Dependencies:** Legal coastal carriage rights depend on review, available vessels, owner participation, ports and product inventory.

**Bottlenecks:** Case-by-case approvals, coastwise carrier supply, high freight and global diesel shortage.

**Substitutability:** Foreign-flag carriers may supplement domestic vessels where approved; equivalence or replacement time is not measured.

**Leverage:** US authorities control eligibility and approval; a wider option does not prove exercised leverage.

**Rent:** Potential freight and price-margin effects are unquantified.

**Adaptation:** A policy extension announced Friday and becoming effective Monday is an institutional adaptation, not a physical delivery on Saturday.

## WHAT CHANGED — AND WHAT DID NOT

**New verified physical system Δ on 14 November: 0.** The evidence supports a decision recorded the previous day, a future effective date, and a conditional route to domestic fuel distribution. It does not support completed 14 November shipments, an automatic drop in diesel prices or a new measured physical fleet.
