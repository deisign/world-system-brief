# Controls meet their workarounds

Two developments reveal different aspects of access control. Shipping data reported by Reuters describe limited Iranian LPG and jet-fuel shipments reaching buyers despite the US naval blockade, including ship-to-ship transfers near Oman. These movements occurred before the article; the date of publication does **not** establish a new shipment today. The physical bottleneck is real, but some routes remain usable for small volumes. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/iran-ships-some-lpg-jet-fuel-despite-us-blockade-data-shows-2026-10-16/).

China has proposed expanding its list of export-controlled technologies, including AI models and agents, computing-cluster technologies, biotech and solar technologies. The draft consultation runs until 15 November; some immediate registration duties for technology transfers have also been reported. The proposed final list and any already effective registration procedure must be treated separately. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/china-proposes-adding-40-ai-biotech-solar-technologies-export-control-list-2026-10-16/) · [WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/economy/trade/china-proposes-export-controls-on-ai-technologies-75ac2030).

## SYSTEM LEDGER

**Energy / logistics.** Flow: LPG and jet fuel from Iran. Bottleneck: US maritime enforcement and access to ordinary shipping services. Alternative: concealed transfers and limited sanctioned-vessel routes. Leverage: enforcement agencies and maritime intermediaries; the report suggests circumvention, not collapse of the blockade. The volume and routes require continued monitoring.

**Technology / data.** Flow: AI know-how, software, models and related technical IP across borders. Proposed bottleneck: Chinese authorization for specified technology exports. Substitution: API-mediated access may remain possible; the regulatory reach over licensing, deployment and source transfer differs. Potential leverage shifts toward licensing agencies and firms controlling portable model IP. A proposal is not final implementation.

**Previously tracked:** Hormuz navigation risk, shadow-fleet service access, US Gulf offshore shut-ins, US PERM restrictions and Yandex data-center disruptions remain on the ledger. No newly verified 16 October state transition is assigned to these older processes.
