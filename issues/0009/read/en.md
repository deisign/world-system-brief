# Recovery is asymmetric

The Gulf energy system is recovering, but not evenly.

New September shipping data put oil flows from Gulf producers excluding Iran at about 81% of their pre-war level. Crude and condensate recovered much further — to 91% — while refined products remained near 60%. That gap is more important than the headline recovery number.

It confirms the trajectory visible since 2 October. The original chokepoint has become partly manageable through route adaptation, dark transits and logistics. But the constraint has migrated downstream. Diesel, jet fuel and other refined products remain harder to replace than crude barrels.

The G7 emergency release is now part of that adaptation layer. Stronger Middle Eastern exports plus the 100 million barrel stock commitment eased immediate market concern today. That is implementation of an earlier delta, not a new one.

A separate regulatory signal came from Brussels. Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said methane exporters would receive one additional year of flexibility. We do not yet have evidence of the formal legal change, so this remains DEVELOPING rather than a confirmed transition.

Ukraine also announced details of a $116.2 million CIF renewable-energy integration plan, including the Chernivtsi–Suceava interconnector and a 610 MW battery-storage window. Available reporting dates the board endorsement to 2 October. WSB therefore records it as late evidence, not as a 6 October delta.
