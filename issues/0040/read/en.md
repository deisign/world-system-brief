# Crude recovers faster than fuel

A market can look balanced in barrels while remaining unbalanced in useful products. On 6 November Reuters assembled two kinds of evidence that must not be conflated: bank estimates of a recovering **global crude-oil balance**, and vessel-tracking estimates showing **refined-product exports from the Gulf recovering much more slowly than crude**. There is no independently established new global refining capacity, pipeline opening or permanent closure today.

## 1. Surplus is an estimate of an earlier month, not today's physical event

JPMorgan, as reported by Reuters on **6 November**, estimated oil supply exceeded demand by **1.1 million barrels a day in September** and **1.4 million in October**. Goldman Sachs separately estimated that global oil inventories stopped falling over September–October after substantial earlier draws. These are retrospective analysts' estimates, not a measurement that the world's oil system acquired 1.4 million barrels a day of new capacity on 6 November.

**System reading:** demand destruction caused by expensive oil, production outside the Gulf and recovering maritime exports can improve the aggregate balance without solving particular downstream shortages. A balance does not show where the remaining constraints sit.

Source: Reuters, 6 November: https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/high-oil-prices-curb-demand-fresh-signs-glut-appear-2026-11-06/

## 2. The key divergence: crude versus refined products

According to Vortexa shipping estimates quoted in Reuters' **6 November** analysis and its earlier **3 November** report, October Gulf exports excluding Iran were approximately **14.0 million barrels a day of crude and condensate**, about **86% of pre-war levels**, but only **3.1 million barrels a day of refined products**, just **over half** of the normal level.

The units are comparable (barrels per day), but the products, denominators and functions differ. These are not a single matched production-to-consumption balance. Missing automatic vessel-tracking signals may lead to upward revisions, and Kpler's regional coverage is not identical.

**System reading:** crude tankers returning does not by itself restore refining, regional fuel availability or product logistics. A supply network is a chain of specialised capacities, and its weakest functioning segment can remain constrained after another segment recovers.

Sources: Reuters, 6 November: https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/high-oil-prices-curb-demand-fresh-signs-glut-appear-2026-11-06/ ; Reuters, 3 November: https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/gulf-oil-flows-dip-october-post-iran-war-high-data-shows-2026-11-03/

## 3. Oil-price curves are signalling different things at different horizons

Reuters reports that prompt **Brent contracts-for-difference swaps** moved into **contango**: very near delivery is priced below a following period. Yet **Brent futures beginning January 2027** retained **backwardation**. The opposing structures apply to **different contracts and dates**; neither implies fuel is equally abundant at every destination.

**System reading:** a perceived near-term crude surplus can coexist with persistent geographic, product-specific and longer-horizon risks. Financial pricing, inventories and actual product shipments must be registered as separate observations.

Source: Reuters, 6 November: https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/high-oil-prices-curb-demand-fresh-signs-glut-appear-2026-11-06/

## 4. Earlier-day context: China also protects its diesel buffer

A **5 November** Reuters report quoted an outlook for **China's November diesel exports below 800,000 metric tonnes**, while commercial diesel stocks at end-October were reported at **11.1 million tonnes**, a low for the series beginning in 2011. These are a *forecast for November* and a *stock observation for October*, not final November shipments. The regional context reinforces the distinction between selling additional fuel abroad and protecting domestic availability; it does not prove that China's shortages caused the Gulf export gap.

Source: Reuters, 5 November: https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/chinas-november-diesel-exports-ease-amid-thin-domestic-stocks-sources-say-2026-11-05/

## SYSTEM LEDGER · The six dimensions

**Flows:** Global crude oil balance; Gulf crude and condensate exports; refinery product exports; Chinese diesel inventories and expected exports.

**Dependency and bottleneck:** Producing crude is not equivalent to refining it and supplying the required fuel in the required market. Shipping access, refining throughput and product storage form distinct network segments.

**Substitutability and horizon:** No reliable common replacement horizon for Gulf refined-product flows, China diesel availability or refinery substitution is established by these reports. Keep **unknown** rather than transferring the UK's mineral-substitution horizon from WSB-0039.

**Leverage:** Product suppliers, refineries and distribution bottlenecks may retain access/pricing influence even when the global crude-oil balance improves. Today's evidence does not measure this leverage causally.

**Rent and earnings:** Contract curves and product scarcity may signal price differences, but they do not directly quantify economic rent or assign it to a particular refiner, shipowner or state.

**Adaptation:** Restoring crude shipments, increasing non-Gulf output and changing consumption are reported adjustments. Effective restoration of Gulf refined-product exports remains incomplete on the reported October comparison; any proposed alternate path needs its own throughput evidence.
