# A TWENTY-YEAR LNG CONTRACT. A ONE-DAY SHIPPING CASUALTY.

The November 18 edition separates three clocks: an LNG agreement signed today for future deliveries, a physical tanker attack that occurred on **November 17** but was confirmed today, and private US oil-inventory estimates for the week ending **November 13**. The contrasts matter: future capacity, reported accident and historic stocks cannot be added into a current supply total. [WSB-0050-SRC-01; WSB-0050-SRC-02; WSB-0050-SRC-03]

## 1. China Gas: contract rights increase before a single extra delivery

China Gas said it signed a revised **20-year LNG supply deal** with US exporter Venture Global at the APEC CEO Summit. The agreement increases annual contracted volume from **500,000 tonnes** under a September contract to **1 million tonnes**, with deliveries expected **as early as 2030**. The company says its overall long-term contracted LNG portfolio reaches **4 million tonnes/year**. This is a real **contractual expansion on 18 November**. It is neither an additional million tonnes of LNG produced in 2026 nor a replacement for a specific stalled Hormuz cargo. [WSB-0050-SRC-01]

## 2. ADNOC: confirmed vessel attack belongs to November 17

ADNOC Logistics & Services said on **18 November** that one of its vessels was attacked in the Strait of Hormuz **the previous day**, leaving **11 crew injured and one missing**, with the others evacuated. Reuters cited security sources identifying an LPG vessel abandoned after a projectile strike. The company did not name the vessel in its statement; wider Iranian claims about multiple oil and LNG tankers on fire were **not independently verified**. A physical security incident occurred, but its proper event date is **17 November** and the data do not substantiate the amount of cargo destroyed or a new route-capacity percentage. [WSB-0050-SRC-02]

## 3. US inventories: a weekly survey is not today's oil delivery

Reuters reported market sources citing the American Petroleum Institute for the week ended **13 November**: US crude stocks rose by **5.28 million barrels**, gasoline by **5.12 million**, and distillates by **351,000**. The numbers are preliminary industry figures, not EIA official statistics. On **18 November at 07:45 GMT**, Brent and WTI futures fell while the report described persistent Hormuz constraints and restricted arbitrage for US crude to Asia and northwest Europe. Prices, private stocks and safe transit are different measurements. [WSB-0050-SRC-03]

## SYSTEM LEDGER · Seven dimensions

**Flows:** China Gas's prospective US LNG purchases from 2030; a November 17 Hormuz vessel incident; US reported petroleum inventories for the week ending November 13.

**Dependencies:** Long-term gas demand depends on LNG capacity, liquefaction, vessels, contracts and receiving terminals. Maritime delivery depends on a safe route; oil inventories depend on storage location and market access.

**Bottlenecks:** Hormuz vessel security and risk acceptance; LNG contract-to-commissioning interval; US Gulf crude price spreads and export arbitrage.

**Substitutability:** US-sourced LNG can diversify future supply but cannot be presumed to replace a particular 2026 Gulf cargo, or to be delivered before 2030.

**Leverage:** A binding longer-term offtake may alter options for buyer and seller, while shipping threats create physical constraints; no numeric leverage change is established.

**Rent:** Future LNG contract economics, freight, insurance and crude arbitrage can change margins; no actor-level net-rent amount is established.

**Adaptation:** China Gas increases a future contractual option; ADNOC evacuates a struck vessel's crew; traders reassess prices after prior-week US stock estimates and security events.

## WHAT CHANGED — AND WHAT DID NOT

**New contractual change on 18 November: confirmed. New attack physically occurring on 18 November: not established.** The ADNOC incident is dated **17 November**. The API-reported inventory build is dated to the **week ending November 13**. Neither is booked as a new commissioned supply or destroyed tonnage today.
